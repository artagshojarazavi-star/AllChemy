============================================================

Build AllChemy: a polished, university-level, offline-first chemistry application for Android phones. It should be installable as an APK, usable on the owner's Android phone, and remain useful long-term without depending entirely on internet services. Product name: AllChemy
Platform priority: Android phone first
Delivery goal: downloadable, installable APK; maintain buildable source code in GitHub
Core principle: local chemistry data and deterministic calculation engines first; online sources and AI augment them rather than replace them.
Quality principle: scientific accuracy, explicit uncertainty, traceable sources, clear units and conditions, and a polished mobile UX.

The owner has authorized the developer to make reasonable product, design, navigation, and feature decisions. Ask the owner before major changes involving privacy, paid services, credentials, deployment, data deletion, or a major product direction.

3. FUNCTIONAL PRODUCT SPECIFICATION
============================================================

A. COMPOUND AND SUBSTANCE SEARCH
- Search by common name, IUPAC name, formula, CAS number where available, synonym, or identifier.
- Example searches: water, vitamin E/tocopherols, sulfuric acid, ammonium, carbonate, glucose.
- Show identity, synonyms, identifiers, formula, molar mass, composition, charge, phase where relevant, and source/provenance.
- Distinguish measured data, curated reference data, computed estimates, and AI-generated explanations.
- Support elements, neutral compounds, ions, and radicals where feasible. Support biomolecules when data exists. Mixtures need separate, scientifically meaningful handling.

B. CHEMICAL STRUCTURES AND VISUALISATION
- 2D skeletal/line-angle drawings, full/displayed structural formulas, Lewis structures (lone pairs, formal charges, resonance forms where appropriate), and simplified diagrams.
- Rotatable 3D viewer with zoom and selectable ball-and-stick, stick, space-filling, and wireframe styles.
- Show/measure bond lengths, bond angles, and torsional/dihedral angles when suitable coordinates are available.
- Explain geometry, molecular shape, hybridisation where appropriate, sigma/pi bonds, polarity, and stereochemistry.
- A molecular formula may correspond to many isomers. Never imply a unique structure when the formula alone does not determine one; ask the user to choose or show plausible alternatives.
- Label 3D coordinates as experimental, retrieved, or generated/estimated.

C. COMPOUND PROPERTIES
- Formula, relative molecular mass/molar mass, elemental percentages, charge, oxidation states where definable, bonding, geometry, functional groups, polarity/dipole information where available, hydrogen-bond donors/acceptors, intermolecular forces, acid/base behaviour, and pKa with conditions/source.
- Solubility, density, melting/freezing and boiling points, vapour pressure, logP/logD, topological polar surface area, and other supported descriptors.
- Physical properties: appearance, phase, conductivity and other values when reliable data exists.
- Chemical properties: common reactions, stability, incompatibilities, decomposition, acid/base reactions, redox behaviour, and reaction conditions.
- Spectral data/predictions where supported: IR and NMR. Clearly distinguish predictions from experimental spectra.
- Thermodynamic properties where reliable data exists: standard enthalpy/entropy/Gibbs energy, heat capacity, formation data, and temperature/phase basis.
- Missing values must say “unknown/not found” rather than be fabricated. Display units, reference conditions, uncertainty, and source.

D. REACTION WORKBENCH
- Accept two or more reactants/products or a natural-language reaction description.
- Suggest plausible products only when a rule or data source supports them; request missing conditions or clarify ambiguous cases.
- Balance equations with atom and charge conservation; support full ionic and net ionic equations where appropriate.
- Support physical states (s), (l), (g), (aq), reversible arrows, catalysts, heat/light/electrolysis, and conditions.
- Reaction details: reactants/products, balanced equation, reaction class, conditions, catalyst, temperature/pressure/solvent when known, observations (colour change, gas, precipitate, temperature change, etc.), hazards/safety, mechanism where known, limiting reagent/stoichiometry, theoretical yield, and thermodynamic data.
- Enthalpy change must state whether experimental, calculated from formation enthalpies, estimated from bond enthalpies, or otherwise inferred. Show method, units, phase and conditions. Never invent ΔH just because an equation is balanced.
- Product prediction is inherently difficult; label rules-based suggestions and uncertain outcomes clearly. Do not promise universal prediction.

E. CALCULATION TOOLS
- Equation balancer, ionic equation simplifier, molar mass, percentage composition.
- Stoichiometry, limiting/excess reagent, theoretical/percentage yield, concentration/dilution, gas calculations, empirical/molecular formula.
- pH/pOH, strong acid/base calculations, buffers and equilibrium calculators within stated assumptions.
- Equilibrium constants, ICE tables, Kc/Kp, reaction quotient where supported.
- Thermochemistry/calorimetry, Hess cycles, bond-enthalpy estimates, Gibbs energy and equilibrium relationships.
- Redox oxidation states, half-equations, electrochemical cells, Nernst equation where supported.
- Spectroscopy aids, unit conversion, significant figures, uncertainty propagation, graph/table utilities.
- Organic chemistry reaction map and functional-group identification.
- Every calculator should show formula, units, assumptions, intermediate steps, and validation messages.

F. LEARNING AND STUDY
- Ask the AI about the compound or reaction currently open.
- Step-by-step explanations with selectable beginner, school/A-level, and university-level depth.
- Revision cards, quizzes, exam practice, worked examples, topic maps, saved notes and favourites.
- Source-linked answers for factual/reference claims where possible.
- Explain why an answer is correct and flag common misconceptions.

G. NAVIGATION AND USER EXPERIENCE
- Polished, responsive, native-feeling mobile design; clear hierarchy and search-first home screen.
- Suggested primary areas: Home/Search, Structures, Reactions, Calculators, Periodic Table, Learn/AI, Saved/History, Settings.
- Search should tolerate common names, formula notation, charges, subscripts and synonyms.
- Use tabs/sections on detail pages so large data sets remain navigable.
- Dark/light theme if practical, accessible contrast, readable chemical notation, and touch-friendly controls.
- Offline status indicator, source labels, data freshness, and graceful empty/error states.
- Avoid one enormous, unstructured wall of data.

4. AI ASSISTANT REQUIREMENTS
============================================================

The assistant is a major differentiator and must be context-aware: it should know which compound/reaction is open and call deterministic calculation tools instead of guessing numerical answers.

OFFLINE MODE
- Answer using bundled reference data and local chemistry engines.
- Perform supported calculations locally and show workings.
- Clearly state when the question requires missing data, current online information, or an LLM unavailable offline.
- Do not pretend a deterministic rules engine is a general-purpose language model.

ONLINE MODE
- Optional provider integration; the previous plan mentioned an Anthropic/Claude API.
- Configure any API key through Settings; never hardcode or commit it.
- Direct API calls from a phone can expose a key to anyone able to inspect the app/device. A secure server-side proxy is preferable for a public release but may add hosting/operating costs.
- The assistant should use structured tools for balancing, formula parsing, stoichiometry, thermodynamics, pH, structure metadata, and compound/reaction lookup.
- Separate source facts from calculations and explanatory text; cite data sources where possible and state confidence/limitations.
- Include conversation history and per-compound/reaction context if practical.
- Do not send private notes or user data to a provider without clear user awareness/consent.

PROPOSED ONLINE SOURCES (VERIFY APIs, TERMS, RATE LIMITS, LICENSING)
- PubChem for compound identifiers, properties, and structures.
- NCI/CADD Chemical Identifier Resolver for name/structure resolution.
- ChEBI and ChEMBL where relevant.
- RCSB PDB for biomolecular structures.
- Wikipedia/Wikidata as supplementary context, not the sole authority for critical values.
- OpenAlex/Crossref for scholarly-paper metadata.
- Reputable thermodynamic/chemical reference sources where licensing permits.
- Internet access fills gaps; it must not be the only data source. Cache suitable results and show provenance/update time.
